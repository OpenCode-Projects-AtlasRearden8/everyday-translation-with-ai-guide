# The Verification Checklist

Print this page or copy it onto a card that lives with your paperwork. It is
the single most-used artifact of this manual: a repeatable pass to run
between "AI gave me a translation" and "I act on it."

## Checklist: A translation you are about to act on

**Step 0 — Safety (before translating at all)**

- [ ] Personal details removed or replaced (names, IDs, addresses, numbers:
      see Chapter 6's red list)
- [ ] Correct tool for the risk level (local/offline for anything sensitive;
      a cloud tool only after de-identification)
- [ ] Document type identified (notice / bill / deadline / legal demand)
- [ ] Sender trusted (real letterhead, expected organization, contact path
      visible)

**Step 1 — Structure (Chapter 1)**

- [ ] I can say in one sentence what this document is and wants
- [ ] Deadline (if any) found and highlighted
- [ ] Amount(s) due (if any) found and highlighted
- [ ] Action item named: pay, reply, appear, apply, or nothing

**Step 2 — Translation**

- [ ] Translated with the original beside it (side-by-side view if possible)
- [ ] Anchor terms kept in both languages (deadline, amounts, medicine or
      meter terms, action verbs)

**Step 3 — Verify (Chapter 7's layers; use as many as the stakes need)**

- [ ] Layer 1 — Sense check: shapes match, numbers match one-by-one, no
      missing or invented paragraphs
- [ ] Layer 2 — Anchor spot-check of 3–5 critical terms in a second tool
- [ ] Layer 3 — Back-translation round trip on any sentence I will act on
- [ ] Layer 4 — Human spot-check for anything with money, housing, health,
      or government stakes

**Step 4 — Act or ask (Chapters 8–9)**

- [ ] If stakes are Tier 3 (court, eviction, health decision, anything
      signed): human path arranged before acting on the translation
- [ ] Notes written: what I verified, when, and (if calling) the office
      answer with date and agent name
- [ ] Paper filed with the letter, note on top: "Checked [date]: deadline,
      amount, contact"

## The 30-second version

For routine, low-stakes paper (a school flyer, an ad), the long list
collapses to three questions:

1. **Is this for real?** (Sender + contact path.)
2. **What do I have to do, and by when?**
3. **Do the dates and amounts match between original and translation?**

If a document fails any of the three, escalate: more layers of the verification pass, human in.

## Why a checklist and not judgment

Beginners (and tired parents at 10 p.m.) make their best decisions with a
list in front of them, not with vibes. The checklist is deliberately short,
deliberately repeated, and deliberately boring. Boring is what a verification
process should feel like. When every box is checked, act with confidence —
and enjoy the calm that comes from knowing, rather than hoping.
