# Privacy First: Keeping Personal Details Out of Cloud Tools

This chapter is the household rulebook that lets you use AI tools without
handing your family's data to strangers. It exists because consumer AI is a
*cloud* service: text you paste travels to a server, may be stored, and may
be reviewed. You would not mail your lease to a stranger. The same instincts
apply here.

## The red list: never paste these

Regardless of which tool, plan, or setting you use, never paste into any
cloud AI tool:

- Full legal names of household members (especially children)
- Addresses, apartment numbers, or a photo that shows your nameplate or box
- Social security / national ID / insurance numbers of any kind
- Account numbers, case numbers, patient IDs, student IDs, badge numbers
- Signatures, ID cards, passports, birth certificates (as images or text)
- Dates of birth, phone numbers, email addresses
- Anything you would be uncomfortable finding reposted somewhere

**De-identify, then translate.** Replace each red-list item with a
placeholder: `[NAME]`, `[ID NUMBER]`, `[ADDRESS]`. Dates of events and
amounts are usually fine to keep — an amount due is not a secret; who owes
it is.

## The green list: usually fine (still verify your instinct)

- Department names ("finance office," "hiring team")
- Document type ("final reminder," "section 4 of the renewal form")
- Amounts, deadlines, and general dates
- The translated text you want to check, with the original term alongside

## Layered defenses, from strongest to lightest

1. **Local AI (nothing leaves the device).** Free, offline tools run small
   language models straight on your computer or phone. Translation quality
   is lower than the big cloud models but is improving quickly, and nothing
   ever leaves your home. Local-first for the web's smallest footprints:
   this is the right tool for anything with red-list risk you cannot strip,
   like a photo you must show but cannot redact.
2. **Offline machine translation.** Phone-based translation apps with a
   downloadable offline language pack translate with no internet at all.
   Great first pass for short texts.
3. **Cloud AI with data controls off.** If you use a cloud tool: turn off
   chat-history/model-training sharing in the settings, use a fresh or
   logged-out session where practical, and still apply the red list.
4. **Redaction instead of translation.** Sometimes the answer is not
   translating the risky part but *not* translating it. Blackout the
   identifiers, translate the rest.

## Photos are documents too

A photo of a document pasted into an AI chatbot is the whole document as far
as privacy is concerned — the tool reads it with the same freedom it gives
text. Photograph with the name and ID covered by your finger or a sticky
note whenever the task allows it, or use local/offline tools for photos of
official documents.

## The practical household rule

When in doubt, use the **Two-Question Test** before pasting:

1. "Does this contain anything on my red list?" If yes: de-identify or go
   local/offline.
2. "Would I be OK with a stranger in another country reading this?" If no:
   de-identify or go local.

It takes three seconds and covers almost every case this manual will send
your way.
