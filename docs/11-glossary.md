# Glossary

Plain-English definitions, the way a friend would explain them. Terms are
alphabetical; the ones in **bold** appear throughout this manual.

**Anchor term** — A word or number so important that a mistranslation
changes what you do: a deadline, an amount due, a medicine name, a unit
("kWh," "mg"). Manual habit: keep anchor terms in both languages when
translating, and check them separately in a second tool.

**Back-translation (round trip)** — Asking an AI to translate its own
translation back into the original language. If new meaning appears in the
round trip, the model invented or softened something. A Layer 3
verification tool.

**Certified translation** — A translation produced by a human professional
who is accountable for its accuracy, sometimes with a signed statement or
seal. Required or expected for signing, courts, and many government uses.
Not something an AI chat provides.

**Cloud AI tool** — Any AI that runs on someone else's computers via the
internet. Capable and convenient; anything you paste travels outside your
home. Chapter 6's rules apply.

**Consent form** — A document you sign to say you were informed and agree.
In healthcare and law. Always a human-translator/interpreter moment,
never an AI one.

**De-identification** — Removing or replacing personal details with
placeholders (`[NAME]`, `[ID NUMBER]`) before text or photos go to a cloud
tool. The three-second habit this manual is built on.

**Disconnection warning (final notice)** — A letter saying service (power,
water, phone) will stop unless you pay or call by a date. Same-day action:
call the utility's office.

**Interpretation** — Spoken-language help (a person talking for you, live).
What you ask for at appointments and offices. See also: right to
interpreter.

**Layered verification** — This manual's four verification layers: (1)
sense-check, (2) anchor-term spot-check, (3) back-translation, (4) human
spot-check. Use deeper layers for higher stakes.

**Local AI (offline AI)** — AI that runs entirely on your own computer or
phone, with no internet in the loop. Nothing leaves your device. Slower and
sometimes less fluent than cloud AI, but the strongest privacy option for
sensitive text.

**Machine translation** — Any translation done by software, AI-based or
older-style. Useful and imperfect. Never accountable in the way a certified
human translator is.

**Notice** — A letter whose purpose is to inform (an event, a change, a
date). Compared with a bill (pay) or a demand (act or face consequences).

**Placeholder** — The bracketed token you substitute for real personal
data: `[STUDENT ID]`, `[ADDRESS]`. Keeps context for the AI while keeping
the data home.

**Route/appeal path** — The contact option in a document: who to call,
write, or visit, and by when if you disagree. A document with no contact
path deserves extra suspicion.

**Red list** — Chapter 6's list of items that never go into a cloud AI
tool: names, ID numbers, addresses, accounts, signatures, birthdates, and
similar.

**Right to interpreter** — In many public services (including most US
healthcare settings under federal language-access rules), you can ask for a
qualified interpreter at no cost. The phrase that does it: "I need an
interpreter for [language]."

**Structure-first reading** — Reading a document's shape (sender, type,
deadline, amount, action, contact) before translating anything. Chapter 1's
core skill, and the reason AI outputs get better.

**Tier 1 / 2 / 3 (stakes)** — This manual's urgency ladder: Tier 1 routine
(read at leisure), Tier 2 money (translate within a day, check amounts),
Tier 3 urgent/legal (same-day human action; translation is support, not
solution).

**Two-Question Test** — Pre-paste privacy check: (1) any red-list item
present? (2) OK with a stranger reading it? If yes-either: de-identify or go
local.

**Verification checklist** — The one-page pass in Chapter 10, run between a
translation and an action. The most-used artifact of this manual.
