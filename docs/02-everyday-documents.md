# Everyday Documents: School Notices and Utility Letters

These are the friendly on-ramps of paperwork translation: low stakes, high
frequency. If your AI setup works well here, the harder chapters will feel
easy.

## School notices

School letters are usually time-sensitive but simple: picture day is Tuesday,
the bus route changed, the flu shot clinic is next week. That mix of urgency
and simplicity is ideal for AI translation.

**Workflow:**

1. Photograph or type the notice (photos often work directly with AI chat
   tools that accept images — check the tool supports image input).
2. Strip personal details: your child's full name, student ID, classroom
   number, teacher's name, your address. Replace them with placeholders like
   `[STUDENT ID]`. The date and the event name can stay.
3. Ask for two things at once:

   > Translate this school notice to [your language]. Then, in one short
   > list, tell me: what is happening, what date it happens, and what
   > (if anything) I need to do before then — like send money, sign a
   > form, or pack something.

4. Sanity-check the date: school notices love relative dates ("this Friday")
   that can slide a week if you misread. If the letter says both a written
   date and a day-name, make sure they agree; if they do not, the letter is
   probably from a previous year.

**Tip for the family translator role:** many households keep a shared note
(even a paper one on the fridge) with a translation of recurring notices:
lunch money day, early dismissal days, parent-teacher conference sign-ups.
Translate the template once with AI, reuse it all year.

## Utility letters

Utility mail comes in four flavors — knowing which one you have solves most
of the problem before translation:

- **The bill.** Look for "amount due," a due date, and a payment section.
  Verify the amount against last month's bill; a sudden triple usually means
  a catch-up adjustment or a meter misread — a good question for the
  utility's office, not a mystery for the AI.
- **The usage statement.** Charts and numbers, no action needed. AI can
  translate the definitions ("kWh," "estimated meter read") once and you can
  reuse that knowledge forever.
- **Service notices.** Planned outages, rate changes, meter upgrade visits.
  These have a date and maybe a "no action needed" line — make sure the AI
  translates *both*.
- **Disconnection warnings.** These are real and urgent: they mean pay or
  call, now. If any letter contains the words *final notice*, *disconnect*,
  *termination*, or *past due*, act the same day — translate enough to grab
  the phone number and the deadline, then call. This is Chapter 9 territory:
  asking a human beats perfecting a translation.

**One-pass prompt for utility mail:**

> This is a letter from a utility company. Tell me: (1) what kind of letter
> this is — bill, usage note, service notice, or disconnection warning;
> (2) any amount due and its due date; (3) any date I need to be home or
> available; (4) the phone number and hours of the customer contact; and
> (5) a plain-language translation of anything else that matters. Use
> headers so it is easy to scan.

## A quick note on junk

Some of the paper is neither school nor utility mail — it is advertising, and
AI can tell you that in three seconds: "Is this a real notice from a company
I do business with, or marketing mail?" Asking that first question saves you
ten minutes a week.
