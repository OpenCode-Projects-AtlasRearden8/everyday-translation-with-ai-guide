# Verifying a Translation Before You Act

The verification pass is what separates "I got a translation" from "I know
what this document says." AI models do not certify their work — and
occasionally they produce fluent, confident, wrong sentences. Fluent and
confident is exactly the wrong combination to trust blindly.

The pass has four layers. Use as many as the stakes require: Layer One for
school flyers, all four layers for anything you would sign.

## Layer 1: Sense-check in your strongest language

Read the translation as if it were written by a slightly strange person and
ask:

- Does it match the *shape* of the original (same number of letterhead
  lines, same paragraph count, same list items)?
- Do the numbers match up one by one: dates, amounts, deadlines, reference
  numbers? Count them. Cross them out as you verify.
- Is there anything in the original the translation does not mention?
  Added paragraphs or missing paragraphs are both red flags.

Expect lots of passes to succeed. That is normal and good: this layer is a
fast filter, not an exam.

## Layer 2: Anchor-term spot-check

Pick the three to five most consequential terms — deadline, amount due,
drug name, action verb, unit ("kWh," "mg," "square meters") — and check each
one:

1. Ask a *second* tool (different company, or an offline dictionary/translator)
   for the same term in isolation.
2. Look up the plain-language definition of the term.
3. Look for the pattern: does the word appear logically (a disconnection
   notice actually talks about payment; an immunization form mentions
   vaccines)?

Mistranslation usually creates nonsense — the pattern breaks. Training
yourself to *notice* broken patterns is most of the verification skill, and
it sharpens quickly.

## Layer 3: Back-translation

Ask the same tool (or a second tool) to translate *its own translation back*
into the original language. Then compare the back-translation to the
original:

> Translate your previous answer back into [original language]. Do not
> look at the original document again — I want an honest round trip.

New meanings that appear in the back-translation are a warning: the model
invented something, softening a demand into a suggestion or vice versa.
Try the round trip on any sentence you will act on.

## Layer 4: Human spot-check

For anything with legal, medical, or financial consequences, have at least
one human who reads both languages — a relative, a friend, a community
worker, or a certified translator — check the same anchor terms. Give them
the *original* plus your translation, ask "do the deadline, the amount, and
the action match?" That 90-second review catches the class of errors no
machine pass reliably catches.

If no such person is available and the stakes are Tier 3 (Chapter 4), the
human office itself is your Layer 4: call, read the deadline aloud, and say
"please tell me I read that right."

## Record what you verified

Keep a cheap habit: in any translation you keep, write at the top (by hand
is fine) the date you verified it and what you checked. "Checked 2026-11-05:
due date, amount, phone." That one line turns a drawer of paper into a
records system, and it is the cheapest insurance this manual offers.
