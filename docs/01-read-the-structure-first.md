# Read the Document's Structure First

## Why structure comes before translation

The most common beginner mistake is pasting a whole document into an AI tool
and reading whatever comes back. It feels efficient. It is not. You have just
asked a machine to guess at context it does not have — and context is
everything in paperwork.

Instead, do what a professional translator does: **read the document's shape
before you translate anything.** Two minutes of structure-reading makes the AI
output dramatically better, because you can tell the AI what the document is,
what part matters, and what to ignore.

Every document in daily life has a skeleton. Learn to spot these six parts:

## The six parts of any letter or form

1. **Header.** Who sent it (letterhead, logo, return address) and when it was
   sent. The header tells you which organization you are dealing with —
   verify the sender is real before trusting the content (scams love official
   letterheads).
2. **Account or case identifiers.** Numbers that tie the letter to *you*:
   account number, case ID, reference number, or a snippet of your address.
   Note these privately; you will want them later, but you will strip them
   before translating (see Chapter 6).
3. **Type of document.** Is this a **notice** (information only), a **bill**
   (money is owed), a **deadline** (something must be done by a date), or a
   **demand/legal letter** (consequences are threatened)? Ask the AI: "What
   kind of document is this: informational notice, invoice, deadline, or
   legal demand?" The answer changes how urgent it is.
4. **The action item.** Almost every real letter has one: pay by, reply by,
   appear on, apply before. Find the date and the requirement *first* —
   everything else can wait.
5. **Dates and amounts.** Circle every date, deadline, and dollar/euro/yuan
   amount. These are the numbers a mistranslation hurts you most, and they are
   exactly what you will double-check in the verification pass.
6. **The contact and appeal path.** Who do you call, write, or visit if you
   disagree or do not understand? If no contact path exists, treat the letter
   with extra suspicion.

## The structure-reading prompt

Copy this into your AI tool along with the document text (with personal
details removed first — see Chapter 6):

> Do not translate this yet. First, describe this document's structure:
> who appears to have sent it, what kind of document it is (notice,
> invoice, deadline, or legal demand), whether there is a deadline or
> amount due, and where a contact or appeal option appears. List the parts
> in order, top to bottom. Keep it under 200 words.

Only after you have read the structure back — and it matches your read of the
paper in your hand — do you move to actual translation in the next step.

## Practice run

Take any old letter from your files (a utility bill works well) and run the
structure prompt on it. Notice how much calmer the document feels once you
know it is "a bill with a due date and a phone number" instead of "a wall of
words." That calm is the point of this whole manual.
