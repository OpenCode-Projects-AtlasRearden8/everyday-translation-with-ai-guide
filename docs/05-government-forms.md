# Government Forms and Official Letters

Government paperwork is the hardest category: fixed deadlines, real
consequences, and forms that assume you already know the system. AI is
genuinely useful here — as a guide and translator, not as the decision-maker.

## What AI can do with government paperwork

- **Explain what the form actually is.** Many forms look like each other.
  "Is this my tax refund notice or a request for more information?" is a
  question AI answers in seconds.
- **Translate instructions field by field.** Working through a form one
  numbered box at a time, with AI translating each instruction, is far
  more reliable than translating the whole page at once.
- **Explain process vocabulary.** "Notice of action," "certified copy,"
  "docket number," "biometrics appointment" — jargon worth one-time
  translations you reuse forever.

## What AI cannot decide

- **Whether you qualify** for a benefit, permit, visa status, or appeal.
- **What to write in a statement or declaration.** These are statements of
  *fact you attest to*. The machine can translate *your* words; it must not
  compose your case for you. Composing is what a lawyer, caseworker, or
  accredited representative is for.
- **The truth of anything with a deadline and consequences.** Court letters,
  tax assessments, and immigration notices have human-lawyer-grade stakes;
  Chapter 9 applies at full strength.

## Working through a form

1. **Get the form page-by-page.** Translate section by section; ask the AI
   to keep the numbering intact so you can follow along on the original:

   > Translate the instructions for Section 2 of this form to [language].
   > Keep the original field numbers (2a, 2b...) and show them next to the
   > translations. Do not fill in anything.

2. **Verify critical data types on paper.** For dates, names, and ID
   numbers, confirm the expected format ("MM/DD/YYYY," "family name first")
   by asking the AI to point to the instruction that says so. Format errors
   cause silent rejections.
3. **Assemble your own checklist.** After the pass, have the AI produce the
   "what I must attach / what I must sign / where I must send it" list —
   then check every item against the original document by eye. The
   attachment list plus the mailing address is where most errors live.

## The office visit trick

Before any in-person government appointment, hand-write a translation card:

- One line stating, in the office's language, who you are and what you need.
- One line stating, in the same language, that you would like an interpreter.
- Your address and phone number, if you choose.

AI can lay out the card text in minutes. Carry it. It turns a stressful
counter interaction into a manageable one. (Note: the card contains personal
data, so it never goes through a cloud AI tool — see Chapter 6.)

## Deadlines

Government deadlines rarely forgive. When a letter announces any deadline:
translate just enough to get the action, the date, and the office phone
number on the same day, call and confirm, and only then do the detailed
reading. Confirming with a live person at the office — asking "what is the
latest date I can reply, and what happens if I miss it?" — is stronger than
any translation.
