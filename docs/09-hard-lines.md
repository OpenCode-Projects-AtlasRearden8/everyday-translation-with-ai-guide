# The Hard Lines: Where AI Translation Must Stop

Every manual has boundaries. This one has four, and they are absolute. They
are not pessimism — they are what makes the rest of this manual safe to use.

## Hard line 1: Never for anything you sign

Do not rely on an AI translation for **anything you will sign**: leases,
contracts, powers of attorney, financial agreements, medical consent forms,
immigration paperwork, employment agreements. An AI translation is a
skilled, fast, unaccountable helper. A signature makes a document legally
yours; the document you sign deserves a human professional's accountability.

What AI *can* do for a document you will sign: highlight which clauses
matter, translate for comprehension, and draft the questions to ask a
lawyer, notary, or certified translator. Use it for that 90% of the journey;
bring in a human for the signature moment.

## Hard line 2: Never for medical decisions

Chapter 3's rule, stated once more for full weight: AI may translate medical
paperwork for understanding. AI may **not** interpret results, recommend
treatment, dose anything, or take any decision role for any medical choice
— yours or a family member's. The right order is always: your health
question → your doctor/nurse/pharmacist → then (optionally) AI to translate
*their* words into your strongest language.

## Hard line 3: Never compose your official statements

Government, court, and immigration paperwork sometimes asks for statements
in your own words. A machine must never write those words for you. Why:
statements of fact are said under penalty and become the record; a composed
statement can drift from the truth in small, plausible ways — and you are
responsible for it, not the software. Translate your *own* drafted words;
let a lawyer or accredited representative help you compose them.

## Hard line 4: Never for emergencies

Fire, flood, medical emergency, police matter: use the emergency number,
use a human interpreter, use any person. Translation software is for calm
moments. In an emergency — call first, translate later.

## If one hard line leaves you stuck

Stuck usually means "you need a person," and people are findable:
- Community organizations and libraries often have free translators/interpreters.
- Legal aid and tenant clinics for housing matters.
- The office on your letter, whose whole job is answering these calls.
- Certified translators for anything you sign.

Asking for help is a skill this manual fully endorses.

## Why these lines make the rest stronger

Notice what these boundaries buy you: because you know you will not rely on
AI for signing, deciding, or emergency paperwork, you can trust AI for
everything else with a light heart. Boundaries are what let beginners move
fast safely — the same reason a bridge has railings. Use the railings; enjoy
the bridge.
