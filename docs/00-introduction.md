# Welcome: AI Translation for Everyday Life

## Who this manual is for

This guide is for multilingual households — families, roommates, and neighbors
who live between two (or more) languages and face a steady stream of paper:
school notices, medical bills, rental letters, utility bills, and government
forms written in a language you did not grow up reading.

It is also for the friend or relative who helps out. Maybe you are the person
in the family who reads English well, and everyone hands you the envelope. This
manual gives you a repeatable, safe way to handle that stack of paper — with AI
doing the heavy lifting and humans making the final calls.

This is **not** a language-learning guide. You will not find vocabulary drills,
conjugation tables, or study plans here. There are excellent guides for that;
this is not one of them. Here, the goal is simply to *understand today's
document* — not to learn the language it is written in. Those are two different
projects, and mixing them up is how people end up with a stack of untranslated
letters and guilt about not "studying harder."

## What AI is good for here

Used carefully, AI is excellent at:

- **Telling you what a document is.** "This is a reminder that your rent is
  due in five days" is a much better starting point than mystery.
- **Translating the everyday stuff.** School event flyers, water bills,
  appointment reminders, parking notices. Machine translation shines here.
- **Explaining structure.** "This letter has three parts: a complaint, a
  deadline, and a phone number to call."
- **Drafting questions.** If you must call the housing office, AI can help you
  write down what to ask, in clear language, before you pick up the phone.

## What AI is not for here

- **Signing anything.** AI translations are good first drafts — they are not
  certified, sworn, or legally binding translations. Never rely on an AI
  translation for a **signed legal or medical decision**. (See Chapter 9,
  "The Hard Lines.")
- **Medical instructions you intend to follow as-is.** Need to know *what* the
  letter says? Ask AI. Need to know *what to do about a health problem*? Ask a
  doctor, nurse, or pharmacist — then use AI only to understand their words.
- **Keeping secrets.** Consumer AI tools run on cloud servers. Anything you
  paste may be stored or reviewed somewhere you cannot see. Chapter 6 shows
  how to strip personal details before anything leaves your house — and when
  to use a tool that never leaves your device at all.

## The golden rule: verify before you act

Every chapter of this manual ends the same way: **the AI gave you a draft;
you check it before you act.** The Verification Checklist (Chapter 10) is the
tool for that. Once you have used it a few times, checking takes minutes — and
it turns "I hope this says my kid has a half day on Friday" into "I know."

## How to use this manual

Read Chapter 1 (read the document's structure first) and Chapter 6 (privacy)
before your first real document. After that, chapters are pick-and-mix: go
straight to the one about the paper in your hand.

The tone of this manual is deliberate: plain English, step by step, and never
condescending. If you are confused by a term, the Glossary at the back explains
it the same way a friend would.
